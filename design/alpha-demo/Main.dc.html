What transparency does to text

Normally text is painted a solid colour and looks the same no matter what sits behind it. The alternative is to make it slightly see-through, so the colour behind bleeds up through the letters and tints them. That is the whole choice. Three demonstrations, loudest first.

1 · The mechanism, turned up loud

The background under both lines goes from strong orange on the left to plain dark on the right. The same sentence is printed twice.

Solid colour
The same grey the whole way across.
See-through
Warm on the left, grey on the right.

The top line ignores the background completely — identical over orange and over dark. The bottom line is the same white at 50% see-through, and it changes as the thing behind it changes. Nothing else differs between the two.

Honestly: the difference is small, and you are not missing anything by finding it hard to spot. The right-hand text is very slightly cooler because the blue glow is coming through it. That is all you get — and all you would get, automatically, under any accent colour a user picks.

See-through means dimmer, and small text has the least to spare. This is the one place I would not use it — which is what the third option, the mix, was about: see-through on the sentences, solid on the little labels.
