Red and green

The red hand is back in all six. Five of them try to get the green in alongside it.

Read the two filtered rows before picking. Around eight percent of men cannot separate red from green, and an icon whose meaning rests on that one distinction says nothing to them. Any version that survives those rows is carrying its meaning in lightness and shape as well as in hue, which is what you want regardless.

At 150px

Red hand

Where we were. Ink sweep on the bubble, flat red on the minute hand, no green anywhere.

Both hands

Ink sweep kept exactly as it is. The hour hand, sitting at 12, goes green; the minute hand goes red. Pass and fail as the two hands rather than as the whole tile.

Through cream

The whole mark sweeps green to cream to red. Putting light between the two hues is what stops them turning to mud where they meet.

Traffic ramp

Green, gold, red across the bubble, hands in cream with a red minute. Green straight into red passes through olive; routing it through gold does not.

Green ring

The mark is green, one hand is red. Dominance instead of balance, which is the usual way this pair is made to work.

Hard split

Cut on the 12 to 6 line, straight through the hour hand. Left of the deadline is green, right of it is red.

On a home screen

Green and red together is the hardest pair in icon design, and the two failure modes are visible above: they go olive where they meet, and they read as a holiday decoration when balanced evenly. Both are avoidable. Neither is automatic.
