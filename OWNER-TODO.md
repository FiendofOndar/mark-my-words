# Owner to-do

Things only the owner can do. Not code, not something a session can finish.
Mostly accounts, paperwork and waiting, which is why they are here: several
have lead times measured in weeks, so starting them late is the expensive
mistake.

A session-start hook prints the unchecked items at the beginning of every
session on this project, so this list reaches the owner without anyone
having to remember where it lives. Tick a box and it stops appearing. The
reasoning behind every item is in [VIABILITY.md](./VIABILITY.md); this file
is the reminder, not the argument.

Last reviewed: 2026-09-15.

## Long lead, start now

- [ ] **Apple Developer Program enrollment.** $99 a year, as an Individual.
      Needs a personal Apple Account in your legal name with two-factor on,
      a personal credit card, a street address (not a P.O. box), and
      possibly a photo of government ID through the Apple Developer app.
      Forum reports from 2026 describe individual enrollments pending from
      two weeks to three months. Nothing about the app is required to start
      this, which is why it goes first. (VIABILITY 4.1a)
- [ ] **Google Play developer account.** $25 once, plus identity
      verification. Also independent of the app being finished. (4.1a)
- [ ] **Play closed testing.** At least 12 testers opted in continuously
      for 14 days before a personal account created after November 2023 can
      apply for production. The 14 days cannot be compressed, so the
      testers need lining up before the build is ready. (4.1a)

## Before the starter pool serves anyone but you

- [x] **Verify the Google spend cap is a real cap.** Confirmed by the owner 2026-09-16: it is a hard cap. Budget alerts notify
      with a delay while the meter keeps running; a hard stop needs the
      native spend cap or a function that disables billing. The project
      notes describe a hard $25 monthly cap, and if that is a budget alert
      it is not protecting you. This is the only protection that does not
      depend on code we wrote. (2.1, 10)

## Needs a live Gemini key, which only you have

- [ ] **Confirm Search grounding works on a free key, and the daily
      limit.** The whole bring-your-own-key path rests on a free key being
      enough. Conflicting snippets exist and Google reportedly removed its
      free-tier table. A "no" here sends the plan to the fallback in 4.4.
      (8.0)
- [ ] **Read the Gemini API terms on third-party apps using a user's own
      key**, and on what the free tier does with prompt content. This
      decides the wording of the consent screen and the privacy policy.
      (8.0a, 8.0g)

## Cheap, do any time

- [ ] **Check both stores for a name conflict** with "Mark My Words", and
      buy a domain. The domain is needed for the privacy policy page and
      the receipt pages regardless. (2.13)
- [ ] **Put the current APK in front of five to ten people.** The build
      already exists. Watch what they do in the first ten minutes and
      whether they open it again in week three. This is the only item in
      the whole plan that can end the project, and the cheapest. (Stage 0)

## After Apple approves the account

- [ ] **Sign the Paid Apps Agreement**, which gates any paid listing and
      any in-app purchase. (4.1a)
- [ ] **Complete the W-9.** Needs your Social Security Number. (4.1a)
- [ ] **Enter banking** in your legal name, exactly as the bank has it.
      (4.1a)
- [ ] **Enrol in the Small Business Program.** 15 percent instead of 30,
      and it is not automatic. (4.1a)
- [ ] **Gather and enter the EU trader details.** A P.O. box with a bill
      showing it is yours, a phone number that can receive a verification
      code, and an email. All three are published on the EU product page.
      A paid app makes trader status certain rather than arguable. (4.1a,
      10)
